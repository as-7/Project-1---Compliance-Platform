SOC 2 — TRUST SERVICES CRITERIA (EXCERPT, ABRIDGED FOR DEMO)

These excerpts are derived from publicly available descriptions of the AICPA
Trust Services Criteria (2017 revision with 2022 updates). They have been
condensed and paraphrased for testing the extraction pipeline and should not
be used as authoritative compliance text. Original criteria are published
by the AICPA at aicpa.org.

================================================================================
CC1.0 — CONTROL ENVIRONMENT
================================================================================

Control CC1.1 — Demonstrate Commitment to Integrity and Ethical Values.

The entity demonstrates a commitment to integrity and ethical values. The
board of directors and management, at all levels, demonstrate through their
directives, actions, and behavior the importance of integrity and ethical
values to support the functioning of the system of internal control.
Standards of conduct are documented in a formal Code of Ethics that is
communicated to all personnel and contractors upon engagement and annually
thereafter. The entity has a defined, documented process to investigate
reports of unethical behavior or violations of the Code. Investigations must
be completed within 30 days. Remediation actions are tracked to closure.
Adherence to the Code is evaluated during annual performance reviews for all
employees including management.

Control CC1.2 — Board Independence and Oversight.

The board of directors demonstrates independence from management and
exercises oversight of the development and performance of internal control.
The board or a delegated committee meets at least quarterly to review the
design and operating effectiveness of internal controls relevant to
security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy.
Meeting minutes are retained for at least five years. The board charter
defines expectations regarding member expertise in information technology
and cybersecurity. Board members complete a conflict-of-interest disclosure
annually.

Control CC1.3 — Organizational Structure, Reporting Lines, and Authority.

Management establishes, with board oversight, structures, reporting lines,
and appropriate authorities and responsibilities in the pursuit of
objectives. Roles related to information security governance are documented
in formal job descriptions. The entity designates a Chief Information
Security Officer (CISO) or equivalent who reports to executive management
no lower than the C-suite. The CISO charter defines authority over policy
approval, risk acceptance, and incident escalation. Segregation of duties
is enforced such that no single individual can both authorize and implement
security-critical changes.

Control CC1.4 — Competence and Accountability.

The entity demonstrates a commitment to attract, develop, and retain
competent individuals aligned with its objectives. Management establishes
competency requirements for key security roles and evaluates whether
individuals meet those requirements through skills assessments and
certifications. Annual training budgets are allocated for the security
function. Performance is evaluated against documented security KPIs at
least semi-annually.

Control CC1.5 — Accountability for Internal Control.

The entity holds individuals accountable for their internal control
responsibilities in the pursuit of objectives. Management communicates
responsibility for internal controls through formal policies and operating
procedures. The entity maintains a responsibility assignment matrix (RACI)
for all security-critical processes, reviewed and updated at least annually.

================================================================================
CC2.0 — COMMUNICATION AND INFORMATION
================================================================================

Control CC2.1 — Internal Communication.

The entity internally communicates information, including objectives and
responsibilities for internal control, necessary to support the functioning
of internal control. Security policies, acceptable use policies, and
incident reporting procedures are made available to all personnel through
the corporate intranet. New employees are required to acknowledge these
policies during onboarding within their first five business days. Any
material revisions to security policies are communicated to all personnel
within 10 business days, and re-acknowledgment is required within 30 days.
Management circulates quarterly security newsletters summarizing threat
trends, metrics, and any policy updates.

Control CC2.2 — External Communication.

The entity communicates with external parties regarding matters affecting
the functioning of internal control. The entity maintains a public-facing
process for responsible disclosure of security vulnerabilities (e.g., a
security.txt file and a bug bounty program). Customer-facing security
documentation, including SOC 2 Type II reports and data processing
agreements, is updated at least annually and made available upon request.
Material security incidents that affect customers are communicated within
72 hours of determination per contractual obligations and applicable
regulations.

Control CC2.3 — Communication of Deficiencies.

The entity evaluates and communicates internal control deficiencies in a
timely manner to the parties responsible for taking corrective action,
including senior management and the board of directors, as appropriate.
All control deficiencies identified through internal audits, penetration
tests, or vendor assessments must be documented in the GRC tracking system
within five business days of discovery and assigned a remediation owner
and target date.

================================================================================
CC3.0 — RISK ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

Control CC3.1 — Specification of Objectives.

The entity specifies objectives with sufficient clarity to enable the
identification and assessment of risks relating to objectives. Security
objectives are documented in the annual security strategy, mapped to
business objectives, and approved by executive management. Objectives
must be measurable and include key risk indicators (KRIs) with defined
thresholds.

Control CC3.2 — Risk Identification and Analysis.

The entity identifies risks to the achievement of its objectives across the
entity and analyzes risks as a basis for determining how the risks should
be managed. The risk register is maintained in the GRC platform and reviewed
quarterly. Each risk is scored on a likelihood-impact matrix (5x5 scale).
Risk assessments consider internal and external threat intelligence feeds,
results from vulnerability scans, and regulatory changes. The annual
enterprise risk assessment includes input from engineering, legal, HR, and
the security team.

Control CC3.3 — Consideration of Fraud Risks.

The entity considers the potential for fraud in assessing risks to the
achievement of objectives. The fraud risk assessment covers unauthorized
access to data, manipulation of financial records, misuse of company
resources, and social engineering threats. Anti-fraud controls include
segregation of duties, transaction monitoring, and whistleblower hotlines.

Control CC3.4 — Identification and Assessment of Significant Change.

The entity identifies and assesses changes that could significantly impact
the system of internal control. Significant changes include mergers and
acquisitions, major infrastructure migrations, adoption of new technologies,
significant regulatory changes, and material changes to the vendor
portfolio. A change impact assessment process is triggered for any event
meeting defined significance thresholds.

================================================================================
CC4.0 — MONITORING ACTIVITIES
================================================================================

Control CC4.1 — Ongoing and Separate Evaluations.

The entity selects, develops, and performs ongoing and/or separate
evaluations to ascertain whether the components of internal control are
present and functioning. Continuous monitoring includes automated
configuration compliance scans (daily), security information and event
management (SIEM) alerting (real-time), and application security scanning
in CI/CD pipelines (per build). Separate evaluations include an annual
SOC 2 Type II audit, annual penetration testing by an independent firm,
and quarterly internal control testing by the compliance team.

Control CC4.2 — Communication of Deficiencies.

The entity evaluates and communicates internal control deficiencies in a
timely manner to those parties responsible for taking corrective action.
Critical findings from audits or assessments are escalated to the CISO
within 24 hours and to the board within five business days. A formal
corrective action plan (CAP) is required within 30 days for all high
and critical findings.

================================================================================
CC5.0 — CONTROL ACTIVITIES
================================================================================

Control CC5.1 — Selection and Development of Control Activities.

The entity selects and develops control activities that contribute to the
mitigation of risks to the achievement of objectives to acceptable levels.
Control design follows a defense-in-depth approach, layering preventive,
detective, and corrective controls. Each control is mapped to one or more
risks in the risk register.

Control CC5.2 — Selection and Development of Technology Controls.

The entity also selects and develops general control activities over
technology to support the achievement of objectives. Technology controls
include access management, change management, operations management, and
security monitoring. These controls are documented in the IT General
Controls (ITGC) matrix and tested at least annually.

Control CC5.3 — Deployment Through Policies and Procedures.

The entity deploys control activities through policies that establish what
is expected and procedures that put policies into action. All policies
are maintained in a centralized policy management system. Policies have a
defined owner, review cycle (at least annual), and approval authority.
Procedures include step-by-step instructions and are version-controlled.

================================================================================
CC6.0 — LOGICAL AND PHYSICAL ACCESS CONTROLS
================================================================================

Control CC6.1 — Logical Access — Identification and Authentication.

Logical access to information assets is restricted through identification
and authentication controls. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required
for all administrative access to production systems, for remote access to
internal networks via VPN, and for access to cloud management consoles.
Passwords must meet minimum complexity requirements: 14+ characters with a
mix of upper, lower, numeric, and special characters. Passwords expire
every 90 days for standard accounts; service accounts use certificate-based
or key-based authentication. Failed login attempts trigger account lockout
after five consecutive failures with a 30-minute lockout period.

Control CC6.2 — Logical Access — Authorization and Provisioning.

Prior to issuing system credentials and granting system access, the entity
registers and authorizes new internal and external users whose access is
administered by the entity. Access is provisioned based on the principle
of least privilege using role-based access control (RBAC). Access requests
require manager approval and, for privileged roles, CISO approval. New
user provisioning is automated through the identity provider. Access is
reviewed quarterly by system owners; orphaned accounts are disabled within
48 hours of detection.

Control CC6.3 — Logical Access — Removal of Access.

The entity removes credentials and access rights for terminated employees
and contractors promptly. Standard user access is revoked within 24 hours
of separation. Privileged access is revoked immediately upon notification.
Shared or generic credentials are rotated within 24 hours after departure
of any user who had knowledge of them. Automated workflows between the
HRIS and identity provider ensure timely de-provisioning.

Control CC6.4 — Physical Access Controls.

The entity restricts physical access to facilities and protected
information assets to authorized personnel. Data center access requires
badge plus biometric verification. Visitor access requires escort and is
logged. Physical access logs are retained for 12 months and reviewed
quarterly for anomalies.

Control CC6.5 — Disposal of Assets.

The entity disposes of information assets in a manner that ensures
confidentiality. Storage media containing sensitive data are sanitized
using NIST SP 800-88 guidelines (purge or destroy) prior to disposal.
Certificates of destruction are retained for each disposed asset.

Control CC6.6 — Encryption in Transit.

The entity protects information assets during transmission over public
and internal networks through the use of encryption. TLS 1.2 or higher
is required for all external-facing services. Internal service-to-service
communication uses mutual TLS (mTLS) where feasible. SSH connections are
restricted to key-based authentication with a minimum key length of 2048
bits. Certificates are managed through automated certificate lifecycle
management and rotated before expiry.

Control CC6.7 — Encryption at Rest.

The entity protects information assets stored on physical media and in
electronic storage through encryption. All databases, file systems, and
object storage containing sensitive or regulated data are encrypted at
rest using AES-256 or equivalent. Encryption keys are managed through a
cloud Key Management Service (KMS) or a dedicated Hardware Security
Module (HSM). Key rotation occurs at least annually and immediately
after any suspected compromise. Key access is restricted to a minimum
number of custodians with split-knowledge procedures.

Control CC6.8 — Protection Against Malware.

The entity implements controls to prevent or detect and act upon the
introduction of unauthorized or malicious software. Endpoint detection
and response (EDR) agents are deployed on all corporate endpoints and
servers. Definitions and behavioral models are updated at least daily.
Email security gateways inspect all inbound messages for malicious
attachments and URLs.

CC7.0 — SYSTEM OPERATIONS
================================================================================

Control CC7.1 — Vulnerability Management.

The entity uses detection and monitoring procedures to identify changes
to configurations that result in the introduction of new vulnerabilities
and susceptibility to newly discovered vulnerabilities. Vulnerability
scanning is performed weekly for internet-facing assets and monthly for
internal assets. Container images are scanned before deployment. Critical
vulnerabilities (CVSS >= 9.0) must be remediated within 15 days of
discovery; high (CVSS 7.0-8.9) within 30 days; medium (CVSS 4.0-6.9)
within 90 days; low within the next planned release cycle. Exceptions
require documented risk acceptance by the CISO.

Control CC7.2 — Security Event Detection and Monitoring.

The entity monitors system components and anomalies that are indicative
of malicious acts, natural disasters, and errors affecting the entity's
ability to meet its objectives; anomalies are analyzed to determine
whether they represent security events. Logs from all production systems
are aggregated in a centralized SIEM. Detection rules cover at least:
brute-force login attempts, privilege escalation, data exfiltration
indicators, configuration changes, and malware indicators. Alerts are
triaged by the SOC with a target response time of 15 minutes for
critical alerts and 1 hour for high alerts. Logs are retained for at
least 12 months online and 24 months in cold storage.

Control CC7.3 — Incident Response.

The entity evaluates security events to determine whether they could or
have resulted in a failure of the entity to meet its objectives (security
incidents) and, if so, takes actions to prevent or address such failures.
The incident response plan defines severity levels (P1-P4) with escalation
timelines. P1 incidents require executive notification within 1 hour.
Post-incident reviews are conducted within five business days of closure.
Root cause analysis is documented and remediation actions are tracked.
Incident response procedures are tested at least annually via tabletop
exercises or full simulations. Lessons learned are incorporated into
the incident response plan and communicated to the organization.

Control CC7.4 — Disaster Recovery and Business Continuity.

The entity tests recovery plan procedures supporting system recovery to
meet its objectives. Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) and Recovery Point
Objectives (RPO) are documented for each critical system. Backup integrity
is verified through automated restore tests at least monthly. Full DR
failover exercises are conducted at least annually, with results documented
and reported to management. Business impact analyses are reviewed annually
and updated whenever significant changes occur to the technology
environment.

Control CC7.5 — Change Management.

The entity authorizes, designs, develops or acquires, configures,
documents, tests, approves, and implements changes to infrastructure,
data, software, and procedures to meet its objectives. All changes to
production systems follow a documented change management process that
includes: change request, impact assessment, peer review, testing in a
non-production environment, approval (at least one reviewer who is not
the author), implementation, and post-deployment verification.
Emergency changes require retrospective documentation within 48 hours.
The change advisory board (CAB) reviews all high-risk changes prior to
implementation.

================================================================================
CC8.0 — CHANGE MANAGEMENT (SUPPLEMENTAL)
================================================================================

Control CC8.1 — Design and Development Controls.

Changes to system components are authorized, designed, developed,
configured, documented, tested, approved, and implemented to meet the
entity's objectives. The SDLC includes requirements gathering, design
review, secure coding standards, automated unit and integration testing,
static application security testing (SAST), and dynamic application
security testing (DAST). Code coverage thresholds are enforced in CI.

================================================================================
CC9.0 — RISK MITIGATION
================================================================================

Control CC9.1 — Risk Mitigation Through Internal Controls.

The entity identifies, selects, and develops risk mitigation activities
for risks arising from potential business disruptions. Business continuity
plans address loss of key personnel, supply chain disruptions, and
extended cloud provider outages. Plans are tested and updated annually.

Control CC9.2 — Vendor Risk Management.

The entity assesses and manages risks associated with vendors and business
partners that could affect the achievement of its objectives. Critical
vendors undergo an annual security assessment that includes SOC 2 report
review, questionnaire-based evaluation, and (for the highest tier)
on-site audit. Vendor contracts include data protection clauses, right
to audit, and incident notification requirements (72-hour SLA).
