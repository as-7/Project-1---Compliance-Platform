HIPAA SECURITY RULE — EXCERPT (ABRIDGED FOR DEMO)

These excerpts paraphrase the HIPAA Security Rule (45 CFR §§ 164.302-318)
and selected provisions of the HIPAA Privacy Rule for demonstration
purposes only. They have been condensed and reorganized for testing the
extraction pipeline. The authoritative text is published by the U.S.
Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) at hhs.gov.

================================================================================
ADMINISTRATIVE SAFEGUARDS (§ 164.308)
================================================================================

§ 164.308(a)(1) — Security Management Process.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures to prevent, detect, contain,
and correct security violations.

Implementation specifications:

(i) Risk Analysis (Required). Conduct an accurate and thorough assessment
of the potential risks and vulnerabilities to the confidentiality,
integrity, and availability of electronic protected health information
(ePHI) held by the covered entity or business associate. The risk analysis
must be conducted at least annually, and whenever significant changes occur
to the environment (e.g., new systems, new business processes, mergers, or
regulatory changes). The risk analysis must identify all systems that
create, receive, maintain, or transmit ePHI; identify and document
reasonably anticipated threats and vulnerabilities; assess current security
measures; determine the likelihood and impact of threat occurrence; and
assign risk levels for all identified risks.

(ii) Risk Management (Required). Implement security measures sufficient to
reduce risks and vulnerabilities to a reasonable and appropriate level.
Risk treatment plans must be documented and include timelines, responsible
parties, and verification criteria. Residual risk must be formally accepted
by an authorized official. Risk management activities must be reviewed
quarterly to assess effectiveness and adjusted as needed.

(iii) Sanction Policy (Required). Apply appropriate sanctions against
workforce members who fail to comply with the security policies and
procedures of the covered entity or business associate. The sanction
policy must define a range of progressive disciplinary actions, from
verbal warning to termination, based on the severity and frequency of
the violation. The policy must be communicated to all workforce members
during onboarding and annually thereafter. All sanctions applied must be
documented in the workforce member's personnel record.

(iv) Information System Activity Review (Required). Implement procedures to
regularly review records of information system activity, such as audit
logs, access reports, and security incident tracking reports. Reviews
must be conducted at least weekly for systems containing ePHI. Anomalies
and suspicious activities must be investigated and documented within
48 hours of detection. Review results must be retained for at least
six years.

§ 164.308(a)(2) — Assigned Security Responsibility.

Standard: Identify the security official who is responsible for the
development and implementation of the policies and procedures required
by this subpart for the covered entity or business associate. The
security official (typically the CISO or equivalent) must have sufficient
authority and resources to fulfill the role. The designation must be
documented and communicated to the entire workforce. The security
official's responsibilities include overseeing risk assessments, managing
the security program, coordinating incident response, and ensuring
compliance with all HIPAA security requirements.

§ 164.308(a)(3) — Workforce Security.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures to ensure that all members of
the workforce have appropriate access to electronic protected health
information (ePHI), and to prevent those workforce members who do not have
access under the applicable section from obtaining access to ePHI.

Implementation specifications:

(i) Authorization and/or Supervision (Addressable). Implement procedures
for the authorization and/or supervision of workforce members who work
with ePHI or in locations where it might be accessed. All access to ePHI
must be authorized by the workforce member's supervisor and the security
official. Authorization must be documented and specify the scope of access
granted, including which systems and what types of ePHI.

(ii) Workforce Clearance Procedure (Addressable). Implement procedures to
determine that the access of a workforce member to ePHI is appropriate.
Clearance procedures include background checks (criminal history, credit
checks for roles with financial access), verification of credentials and
qualifications, and reference checks. Clearance must be completed before
access is provisioned.

(iii) Termination Procedures (Required). Implement procedures for
terminating access to ePHI when the employment of, or other arrangement
with, a workforce member ends, or when an entity determines that access
is no longer needed. Access to ePHI must be revoked within 24 hours of
separation for standard users and immediately for users with privileged
or administrative access. All entity-owned devices and media must be
recovered. Shared credentials known to the departing member must be
changed within 24 hours.

§ 164.308(a)(4) — Information Access Management.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures for authorizing access to
ePHI that are consistent with the applicable requirements of subpart E
(the Privacy Rule).

Implementation specifications:

(i) Isolating Health Care Clearinghouse Functions (Required). If a health
care clearinghouse is part of a larger organization, the clearinghouse
must implement policies and procedures that protect ePHI from unauthorized
access by the larger organization.

(ii) Access Authorization (Addressable). Implement policies and procedures
for granting access to ePHI, for example through access to a workstation,
transaction, program, process, or other mechanism. Access authorization
must be based on the principle of least privilege — workforce members
receive only the minimum access necessary to perform their job functions.
Role-based access control (RBAC) must be implemented where feasible.
Access requests must include documented justification and manager approval.
Privileged access requires additional approval from the security official.

(iii) Access Establishment and Modification (Addressable). Implement
policies and procedures that, based upon the entity's access authorization
policies, establish, document, review, and modify a user's right of access
to a workstation, transaction, program, or process. Access modifications
must be documented with the date, requestor, approver, and specific access
granted or revoked. Access reviews must be conducted at least quarterly for
systems containing ePHI. Any unauthorized access discovered during review
must be revoked immediately and investigated as a potential security
incident.

§ 164.308(a)(5) — Security Awareness and Training.

Standard: Implement a security awareness and training program for all
members of the workforce (including management).

Implementation specifications:

(i) Security Reminders (Addressable). Provide periodic security updates
and reminders to the workforce. Reminders should be distributed at least
monthly and cover current threat trends, phishing indicators, and policy
updates. Reminders may be delivered via email, intranet postings, or
digital signage.

(ii) Protection from Malicious Software (Addressable). Implement
procedures for guarding against, detecting, and reporting malicious
software. All workforce members must be trained to recognize suspicious
emails, links, and attachments. Reporting procedures for suspected
malware must be documented and communicated.

(iii) Log-in Monitoring (Addressable). Implement procedures for monitoring
log-in attempts and reporting discrepancies. Failed login attempts must be
logged and monitored. Alerts must be generated after five consecutive
failed attempts. Repeated failed login patterns must be investigated.

(iv) Password Management (Addressable). Implement procedures for creating,
changing, and safeguarding passwords. Passwords must meet minimum
complexity requirements: at least 12 characters, including upper and lower
case letters, numbers, and special characters. Passwords must be changed
at least every 90 days. Password reuse of the last 12 passwords is
prohibited. Default passwords must be changed immediately upon deployment.

§ 164.308(a)(6) — Security Incident Procedures.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures to address security incidents.

Implementation specification:

(i) Response and Reporting (Required). Identify and respond to suspected
or known security incidents; mitigate, to the extent practicable, harmful
effects of security incidents that are known to the covered entity or
business associate; and document security incidents and their outcomes.
Security incidents must be classified by severity level (Critical, High,
Medium, Low). Critical and High incidents require immediate notification
to the security official and initiation of the incident response plan.
Incident response includes: detection, analysis, containment, eradication,
recovery, and post-incident review. Incident logs must be retained for at
least six years and must include: date and time of detection, description
of the incident, systems and data affected, response actions taken,
personnel involved, and outcome. Post-incident reviews must be completed
within 30 days of incident closure.

§ 164.308(a)(7) — Contingency Plan.

Standard: Establish (and implement as needed) policies and procedures for
responding to an emergency or other occurrence (for example, fire,
vandalism, system failure, and natural disaster) that damages systems that
contain ePHI.

Implementation specifications:

(i) Data Backup Plan (Required). Establish and implement procedures to
create and maintain retrievable exact copies of ePHI. Backups must be
performed at least daily for critical systems. Backup integrity must be
verified through automated validation checks. Backup media must be
encrypted using AES-256 or equivalent. Off-site or cloud backup storage
must be in a geographically separate location.

(ii) Disaster Recovery Plan (Required). Establish (and implement as needed)
procedures to restore any loss of data. The disaster recovery plan must
define Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPO)
for each system containing ePHI. RTO for critical systems shall not exceed
4 hours; RPO shall not exceed 1 hour. The plan must address scenarios
including: complete site loss, partial infrastructure failure, data
corruption, and ransomware.

(iii) Emergency Mode Operation Plan (Required). Establish (and implement as
needed) procedures to enable continuation of critical business processes
for protection of the security of ePHI while operating in emergency mode.
Emergency mode procedures must identify essential functions, alternative
processing facilities, and manual procedures that can sustain operations
until normal operations are restored.

(iv) Testing and Revision Procedures (Addressable). Implement procedures
for periodic testing and revision of contingency plans. Contingency plans
must be tested at least annually through tabletop exercises, functional
exercises, or full-scale tests. Test results must be documented and include
identified deficiencies and corrective actions. Plans must be revised based
on test results, actual incidents, organizational changes, and changes in
the regulatory environment.

(v) Applications and Data Criticality Analysis (Addressable). Assess the
relative criticality of specific applications and data in support of other
contingency plan components. The criticality analysis must identify and
prioritize all applications and data that create, receive, maintain, or
transmit ePHI. Business impact analysis must quantify the operational and
financial impact of system downtime at various durations.

§ 164.308(a)(8) — Evaluation.

Standard: Perform a periodic technical and nontechnical evaluation, based
initially upon the standards implemented under this rule and, subsequently,
in response to environmental or operational changes affecting the security
of ePHI, that establishes the extent to which a covered entity's or
business associate's security policies and procedures meet the requirements
of this subpart. Evaluations must be performed at least annually by
qualified internal or external assessors. Evaluation results must be
documented and deficiencies tracked to remediation.

PHYSICAL SAFEGUARDS (§ 164.310)
================================================================================

§ 164.310(a)(1) — Facility Access Controls.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures to limit physical access to
its electronic information systems and the facility or facilities in which
they are housed, while ensuring that properly authorized access is allowed.

Implementation specifications:

(i) Contingency Operations (Addressable). Establish procedures for
allowing facility access in support of restoration of lost data under the
disaster recovery plan and emergency mode operations plan.

(ii) Facility Security Plan (Addressable). Implement policies and
procedures to safeguard the facility and the equipment therein from
unauthorized physical access, tampering, and theft. The facility security
plan must include perimeter security, access control mechanisms (badge,
biometric, PIN), surveillance systems, visitor management procedures, and
environmental controls (fire suppression, climate control, water detection).

(iii) Access Control and Validation Procedures (Addressable). Implement
procedures to control and validate a person's access to facilities based
on their role or function. Physical access logs must be maintained and
retained for at least six years. Access logs must be reviewed at least
quarterly for anomalies.

(iv) Maintenance Records (Addressable). Implement policies and procedures
to document repairs and modifications to the physical components of a
facility which are related to security. Maintenance logs must include the
date, description of work, personnel involved, and any impact on security
controls.

§ 164.310(b) — Workstation Use.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures that specify the proper
functions to be performed, the manner in which those functions are to be
performed, and the physical attributes of the surroundings of a specific
workstation or class of workstation that can access ePHI. Workstation
policies must address screen locking, clean desk requirements, and
restrictions on accessing ePHI in public locations.

§ 164.310(c) — Workstation Security.

Standard: Implement physical safeguards for all workstations that access
ePHI, to restrict access to authorized users. Physical safeguards include
cable locks, secure office locations, and full-disk encryption on portable
devices.

§ 164.310(d) — Device and Media Controls.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures that govern the receipt and
removal of hardware and electronic media that contain ePHI into and out
of a facility, and the movement of these items within the facility.
Media disposal must follow NIST SP 800-88 guidelines. All disposed media
must be accompanied by a certificate of destruction. Media reuse requires
verified sanitization before redeployment.

TECHNICAL SAFEGUARDS (§ 164.312)
================================================================================

§ 164.312(a)(1) — Access Control.

Standard: Implement technical policies and procedures for electronic
information systems that maintain ePHI to allow access only to those
persons or software programs that have been granted access rights as
specified in § 164.308(a)(4).

Implementation specifications:

(i) Unique User Identification (Required). Assign a unique name and/or
number for identifying and tracking user identity. Shared or group
accounts are prohibited for systems containing ePHI. Each user must have
a unique identifier that is linked to audit trail entries.

(ii) Emergency Access Procedure (Required). Establish (and implement as
needed) procedures for obtaining necessary ePHI during an emergency.
Emergency access must be logged and reviewed within 24 hours. Emergency
access credentials must be stored in a sealed, audited container (physical
or virtual).

(iii) Automatic Logoff (Addressable). Implement electronic procedures that
terminate an electronic session after a predetermined time of inactivity.
Clinical workstations must enforce automatic logoff after no more than
15 minutes of inactivity. Administrative systems must enforce logoff after
no more than 30 minutes. Logoff policies must be enforced via system
configuration, not user discretion.

(iv) Encryption and Decryption (Addressable). Implement a mechanism to
encrypt and decrypt ePHI. Encryption must use FIPS 140-2 validated
cryptographic modules. AES-256 is required for data at rest. TLS 1.2 or
higher is required for data in transit.

§ 164.312(b) — Audit Controls.

Standard: Implement hardware, software, and/or procedural mechanisms that
record and examine activity in information systems that contain or use
ePHI. Audit logs must capture at minimum: user identification, type of
event, date and time, success or failure indication, origination of event,
and identity of affected data or system component. Audit logs must be
retained for a minimum of six years. Audit log integrity must be protected
through write-once storage, digital signatures, or hash chain verification.
Logs must be reviewed at least weekly for security-relevant events. Alerts
must be configured for high-risk events such as privilege escalation, bulk
data access, and failed authentication attempts.

§ 164.312(c) — Integrity.

Standard: Implement policies and procedures to protect ePHI from improper
alteration or destruction.

Implementation specification:

(i) Mechanism to Authenticate ePHI (Addressable). Implement electronic
mechanisms to corroborate that ePHI has not been altered or destroyed in
an unauthorized manner. Integrity controls include digital signatures,
checksums, and version control for structured data. Database integrity
is ensured through referential integrity constraints, transaction logging,
and point-in-time recovery capabilities.

§ 164.312(d) — Person or Entity Authentication.

Standard: Implement procedures to verify that a person or entity seeking
access to ePHI is the one claimed. Authentication mechanisms must include
at least two of the following factors for access to ePHI: something you
know (password, PIN), something you have (token, smart card), something
you are (biometric). Multi-factor authentication is required for remote
access to systems containing ePHI.

§ 164.312(e) — Transmission Security.

Standard: Implement technical security measures to guard against
unauthorized access to ePHI that is being transmitted over an electronic
communications network.

Implementation specifications:

(i) Integrity Controls (Addressable). Implement security measures to
ensure that electronically transmitted ePHI is not improperly modified
without detection until disposed of. Message integrity can be ensured
through TLS, IPSec, or application-level message authentication codes.

(ii) Encryption (Addressable). Implement a mechanism to encrypt ePHI
whenever deemed appropriate. Encryption is required for all ePHI
transmitted over public networks (internet, wireless). Encryption is
strongly recommended for ePHI transmitted over internal networks,
especially across trust boundaries. Minimum encryption standard is
TLS 1.2 with strong cipher suites; TLS 1.3 is preferred.

ORGANIZATIONAL REQUIREMENTS (§ 164.314)
================================================================================

§ 164.314(a) — Business Associate Contracts or Other Arrangements.

Standard: The contract or other arrangement between the covered entity and
its business associate must meet the requirements of paragraphs (a)(2)(i)
or (a)(2)(ii) as applicable.

The business associate contract must provide that the business associate
will: (a) implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards that
reasonably and appropriately protect the confidentiality, integrity, and
availability of the ePHI it creates, receives, maintains, or transmits on
behalf of the covered entity; (b) ensure that any subcontractors that
create, receive, maintain, or transmit ePHI agree to the same restrictions
and conditions; (c) report to the covered entity any security incident of
which it becomes aware, including breaches of unsecured ePHI; (d) authorize
termination of the contract by the covered entity if the business associate
has violated a material term of the contract. Business associate agreements
must be reviewed at least annually and updated when business operations or
regulatory requirements change.

§ 164.314(b) — Requirements for Group Health Plans.

Standard: A group health plan must ensure that its plan documents provide
that the plan sponsor will reasonably and appropriately safeguard ePHI.
Plan documents must be amended to incorporate provisions requiring the
plan sponsor to implement administrative, physical, and technical
safeguards, report security incidents, and ensure that separation between
the group health plan and the plan sponsor is maintained.

POLICIES, PROCEDURES, AND DOCUMENTATION (§ 164.316)
================================================================================

§ 164.316(a) — Policies and Procedures.

Standard: Implement reasonable and appropriate policies and procedures to
comply with the standards, implementation specifications, or other
requirements of this subpart, taking into account the factors specified in
§ 164.306(b). Policies must be written, approved by the security official,
and reviewed at least annually or whenever significant changes occur.

§ 164.316(b) — Documentation.

Standard: (i) Maintain the policies and procedures implemented in written
(which may be electronic) form. (ii) If an action, activity, or assessment
is required to be documented, maintain a written (which may be electronic)
record of the action, activity, or assessment. Documentation must be
retained for a minimum of six years from the date of its creation or the
date when it last was in effect, whichever is later. Documentation must
be made available to those persons responsible for implementing the
procedures to which the documentation pertains. Documentation must be
reviewed periodically and updated as needed in response to environmental
or operational changes.
